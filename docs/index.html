Overview

Two plugins, one booking experience

Appointiva is a free, standalone booking plugin that works on its own. Appointiva Pro is a separate add-on that plugs into Free's extension points to unlock advanced, business-grade features. Pro requires the free plugin to be installed and active.

🗓️ Appointiva Free
Free · WordPress.org · v1.0.0

A complete single-staff booking calendar for salons, consultants, tutors, clinics and service businesses. Multilingual, RTL-ready, and accessible out of the box.

  • Single-service booking calendar
  • Weekly availability & blocked time
  • Automatic confirmation & reminder emails
  • Stripe & PayPal payments
  • Gutenberg block & shortcode
  • GDPR export/erase tools
⭐ Appointiva Pro Pro
Paid add-on · Requires Free · v1.9.0

Everything in Free, extended for teams: multiple staff, WhatsApp, recurring bookings, prepaid packages, deposits, ticketed events, a customer self-service portal, and more.

  • Multi-staff scheduling
  • WhatsApp reminders
  • Recurring appointments & packages
  • Deposits & two-way calendar sync
  • Events with e-tickets
  • Customer dashboard, API, CSV, white-label
How they fit together: Pro never modifies Free's files. It hooks into the actions and filters Free exposes at every step of the booking flow — so when a Pro feature is unlicensed or disabled, Free keeps working exactly as it would on its own (a graceful downgrade, not a hard lock).
Getting started

Install and take your first booking

You can be live in a few minutes with just the free plugin. Add Pro later whenever you need the extra capabilities.

Set up Appointiva (Free)

  1. Install & activateUpload the appointiva folder to /wp-content/plugins/ (or install from the Plugins screen), then activate it.
  2. Add a serviceGo to Appointiva in the admin menu, create your first service (name, duration, price), and assign it to a staff member.
  3. Set your weekly availabilityDefine which days and hours you accept bookings, plus any blocked-off time.
  4. Embed the booking formAdd the Appointiva Booking Widget block, or the [appointiva_booking] shortcode, to any page or post.
  5. Configure email & (optionally) paymentsUnder Notifications set up SMTP for reliable delivery; under Payments add Stripe or PayPal keys if you collect payment.

Add Appointiva Pro

  1. Keep Free activePro checks that the free plugin is present before it boots — install and activate Appointiva first.
  2. Install Pro & activateUpload and activate the appointiva-pro plugin alongside Free.
  3. Enter your license keyOpen Appointiva → License and activate your key. This unlocks the Pro features and enables automatic updates.
  4. Turn on the features you needEach Pro feature has its own screen or settings tab — enable multi-staff, WhatsApp, deposits, and the rest individually.
Requirements: WordPress 6.5+ and PHP 8.1+ for both plugins. Pro requires Appointiva (Free) 1.0.0 or newer.

Booking & availability

Create a service, set your weekly hours, and the widget computes valid slots for visitors automatically. At the moment a booking is placed, availability is re-checked server-side — so the slot shown as free and the slot actually booked can't drift apart under load.

Tip: Blocked-time rules are perfect for lunch breaks, holidays, or one-off closures without changing your recurring weekly hours.

Notifications & email

Confirmation and reminder emails are locale-safe — Appointiva switches to the customer's language and reloads the correct translations before sending, something WordPress doesn't do automatically for plugin text domains. For reliable delivery, enable SMTP under Appointiva → Notifications and pick a provider preset.

Payments

Add Stripe or PayPal credentials under Appointiva → Payments. Both gateways talk to their vendor APIs directly over WordPress's HTTP layer. (The payment step is optional — many businesses run booking-only and collect payment in person.)

Privacy & GDPR

The booking form carries an explicit consent checkbox, and Appointiva registers with WordPress's core personal-data exporter and eraser so subject-access and deletion requests just work. No data is ever sent to an external service until you actively configure and enable an integration (Google Calendar, Stripe, PayPal, or SMTP).

Licensing & updates

Activate your license under Appointiva → License. Pro re-validates daily and includes a 7-day grace period for transient network failures — but a definitive server rejection (expired or revoked) downgrades immediately. Automatic updates only deliver the download when your key and domain resolve to a currently-active activation.

  1. Enter your keyPaste your license key on the License screen and click Activate.
  2. Confirm "Active"The panel shows your activation status; updates now flow automatically.
  3. Manage activationsEach license allows a set number of site activations; deactivate a site to free a slot.

Multi-staff scheduling

Assign multiple staff members to a single service. Available slots are merged and de-duplicated across every candidate, and at booking time the system re-checks and assigns the specific staff member who's actually free — closing the gap between "shown as free" and "actually booked." Assignments are managed on the service, license-gated so the endpoint returns a clear error if your license has lapsed.

WhatsApp reminders

WhatsApp becomes an additional notification channel alongside email. Configure it on the Settings screen with your WhatsApp Business phone number ID and access token, then map a pre-approved message template + language for each notification type.

Important: Business-initiated WhatsApp messages must use a pre-approved template from your Meta Business account. Each Appointiva template needs exactly three body placeholders in a fixed order: customer first name, service name, and formatted appointment date/time. Use the built-in Test button to confirm your setup works before relying on it.

Recurring appointments

From the Recurring screen, an admin books a repeating series on a customer's behalf (weekly, biweekly, or monthly). Every occurrence is a real booking created through Free's own booking engine, so confirmation emails, staff resolution and conflict checks all apply identically. A conflicting occurrence is recorded as skipped with a reason rather than failing the whole series, and cancelling a series only cancels its future occurrences — past ones are left as history.

Packages

A package is a bundle of N prepaid sessions of one service, sold up front and redeemed one at a time. Each redemption creates a real booking; the purchase auto-transitions to exhausted once every session is used. A failed redemption (e.g. a slot conflict) consumes nothing — the customer never loses a paid session to a scheduling clash. Manage definitions and purchases on the Packages screen.

Deposits

Require a deposit (fixed amount or percentage) before a booking is confirmed. A booking for a deposit-required service lands at pending instead of auto-confirming. From the Deposits screen you can mark a deposit paid manually (cash, transfer) or charge it through Stripe/PayPal — the deposit charges less than full price through the same gateway code Free already uses.

Two-way calendar sync

Free pushes bookings to Google Calendar one-way; Pro completes the loop. It tracks each booking's calendar event (deleting it from Google if the booking is cancelled) and pulls the calendar's busy periods hourly, so an external event — a staff member's personal appointment, a holiday — actually blocks that time from being booked here. The Calendar Sync screen shows connection state, last sync time, and a manual "Sync now" button.

Customer self-service dashboard

A customer-facing portal that needs no WordPress account. Add the [appointiva_dashboard] shortcode to a page; customers arrive via a pre-authenticated "Manage this booking" link in their confirmation email, or request a magic link by email. From there they can view their bookings, packages and recurring series, and cancel their own bookings or series — view + cancel only, never destructive to anyone else's data.

Auth model: Stateless, HMAC-signed tokens (a 15-minute magic link and a 30-day session token) signed with your site's own secret — no new credentials, no password to manage.

In-plugin translations

WPML/Polylang already translate your UI strings; this fills the one gap they don't — your own service names and descriptions. On the Translations screen, expand a service and add a per-locale name/description (restricted to locales actually installed on your site). The public services list and booking widget then serve the translated copy on an already-localized page automatically.

External integration API

A separate REST surface for external tools acting with your merchant authority — Zapier, a custom app, a future mobile client. Create an API key on the API Keys screen; the raw key is shown exactly once (only a hash is stored, like Stripe/GitHub). Keys carry a read or read_write scope and can create, list and cancel bookings through Free's real booking lifecycle, tagged source: api.

CSV import/export

Bulk-move customers and bookings in and out via CSV — for migrating from another system or keeping an offline backup. Export supports the same status/date filters as the API. Import calls Free's own creation logic per row, skipping bad rows with a specific reason while the rest of the file still imports. Import is gated behind an explicit "I confirm I have a lawful basis" checkbox.

Events with e-tickets

A separate model from appointments: a fixed-time, multi-attendee gathering sold by ticket type. Ticket sales use an atomic reservation so the last ticket can't be oversold under a race. Check-in looks a ticket up by its human-readable number (EVT-XXXXXX) or QR token, within a one-hour-before-to-event-end window. Cancelling a ticket restores its quantity; an event can't be deleted once tickets have sold. The public purchase route is the one unauthenticated endpoint — everything else is capability- and license-gated.

White-label branding

Cosmetic rebranding of a single site for an agency or reseller. Set a brand name, tagline and logo, and optionally hide the Pro upsell card — the admin sidebar, header, and even the WordPress Plugins-list entries for both plugins are relabeled. When disabled (the default), everything reverts to Appointiva's own branding unchanged.

Scope: This is single-site cosmetic rebranding, not a multi-tenant reseller system that manages several separate client sites.

Elementor widgets

Two native widgets appear under the Appointiva category in the Elementor editor panel:

Booking Form renders Free's exact same booking form (same two-step flow, availability and REST calls — nothing is reimplemented), so behavior stays identical to the block and shortcode. In the Content tab you can preselect a single service (or let the visitor choose) and pick a light/dark theme; the Style tab exposes per-element controls for the container, progress steps, fields & inputs, calendar, time slots, buttons, and summary/messages — colors, typography, borders, spacing and more.

Services Dropdown is a compact "pick a service and go" selector — ideal for a homepage hero or header. It lists every active service (optionally with duration and price) and, on submit, sends the visitor to your booking page with that service already selected. Point its Booking page at the page holding the Booking Form widget; leave it empty to submit to the current page (so a dropdown and form on the same page work together). The dropdown and button are fully style-able.

Live preview: The booking form is fully interactive right inside the Elementor editor — it hydrates as you add or edit the widget, so you can see your style changes against the real form. Like every Pro feature it's license-gated — visitors see nothing when the license is inactive, while an admin previewing the page sees a short notice explaining why.
